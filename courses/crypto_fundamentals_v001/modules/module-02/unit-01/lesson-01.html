Spotting Crypto Scams

Scams exploit urgency and authority more than technology. A wallet provider, support agent, or giveaway should never need your seed phrase.

Likely legitimate

You navigate directly to a verified domain, take time to review, and the service never asks for recovery words. Legitimate flows feel calm — no countdown timers, no “act now or lose funds” pressure.

  • You typed or bookmarked the official URL yourself
  • Support never requests a seed phrase or private key
  • Transaction details match what you initiated
  • You can pause, research, and cancel without losing access
  • Branding and certificates match known official sources
Likely scam

A DM, sponsored ad, or pop-up claims your assets are at risk and demands a phrase, signature, or remote access. Urgency is the tell — scammers collapse your time to think.

  1. Stop interacting — close the tab or chat immediately
  2. Do not enter a seed phrase, approve blind signatures, or install remote tools
  3. Open a fresh browser and type the known official URL yourself
  4. Verify status from the wallet/app you already trust
  5. Report the scam link if your platform provides a channel
Drill the red flags

Cryptographic hash

One-way fingerprint

Maps any input to a fixed-length digest. A tiny change avalanches into a totally different output — perfect for tamper evidence.

Private key

Signing secret

Secret material that authorizes spends. Anyone who holds it can move the associated funds — never share or screenshot it.

Public address

Safe to publish

Derived from keys and used only to receive. Share it like an account number — it cannot authorize an outgoing spend by itself.

Network consensus

Shared history

How peers agree on one ledger tip without a central referee — the foundation of decentralized settlement.

Always-on checklist

  • Reject urgency and guaranteed returns
  • Verify URLs character by character
  • Read every transaction or signature request
  • Keep learning funds small
